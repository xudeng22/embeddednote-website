In its 2025 outlook, IoT Analytics projected that the number of connected IoT devices worldwide would grow 14% to 21.1 billion in 2025, reach 39 billion by 2030 and surpass 50 billion by 2035.

## The three leading IoT connectivity technologies in 2025

![IoT Analytics graphic illustrating global connected IoT device growth](/uploads/state-of-iot-2025-connectivity/state-of-iot-2025-connectivity-01.webp)

### 1. Wi-Fi IoT

Wi-Fi accounts for 32% of IoT connections, making it the largest connectivity technology in the market. After Wi-Fi IoT chipset shipments returned to more normal levels in 2024, three developments are shaping its next phase of growth:

**Lower-power Wi-Fi for battery-powered devices.** Features introduced with Wi-Fi 6, notably Target Wake Time and extended sleep modes, help reduce energy use. That makes Wi-Fi more practical for battery-operated sensors, smart locks and appliances.

**Enterprise network upgrades.** Businesses are replacing older equipment with Wi-Fi 6E and Wi-Fi 7, improving throughput, latency and reliability. Upgrades to customer-premises equipment, edge devices, broadband gateways and fixed wireless access (FWA) infrastructure are supporting demand.

**Longer-range industrial links.** Wi-Fi HaLow (IEEE 802.11ah) operates below 1 GHz and is gaining traction where conventional Wi-Fi range is limiting. Potential applications include industrial video sensors, advanced metering infrastructure (AMI 2.0) and precision agriculture. IoT Analytics expects this market to contribute to shipment growth from 2026 onward.

### 2. Bluetooth IoT

Bluetooth represents 24% of connected IoT devices worldwide, making it the second-largest connectivity category. After chipset shipments normalized in 2024, Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) remained a key option for battery-powered equipment. Newer SoC families—including Nordic Semiconductor's nRF54, Silicon Labs' BG27 and Texas Instruments' CC23xx—combine processing, radio and security functions while targeting lower power consumption and cost. The report also points to growing industrial interest in IO-Link Wireless for reliable links among sensors, actuators and controllers. Bluetooth 5.4 is finding a role in large electronic shelf-label deployments in North America and Europe. Meanwhile, Bluetooth Channel Sounding enables more precise, secure distance measurements for access systems, real-time location services (RTLS) and digital car keys, with applications across hospitals, factories and warehouses.

### 3. Cellular IoT

Cellular technologies—including 2G, 3G, 4G, 5G, LTE-M and NB-IoT—account for 22% of global IoT connections. IoT Analytics' September 2025 connectivity tracker reported that cellular IoT connections grew 16% year over year in 2024, faster than the overall IoT market.

The firm's *Wireless IoT Connectivity Chipset Market Report 2025–2030*, issued in October 2025, put the cellular IoT chipset market at **$4.07 billion in 2024**, up 19%. It projected **$14.08 billion by 2030**, a 23% compound annual growth rate. The report identified 5G chipsets as a major contributor, forecasting **$9.31 billion in 2030** and a 34% CAGR. Demand drivers include FWA, video telematics, industrial gateways and automotive systems, with private 5G deployments in China illustrating the technology's potential at scale.

IoT Analytics highlights three technology shifts likely to shape cellular IoT growth:

**5G:** Increasingly used for applications requiring low latency and reliable connectivity, including FWA, video telematics, industrial gateways and automotive systems. Large-scale private 5G networks in China offer examples of deployment at scale.

**LTE Cat-1:** Still a widely available, cost-effective choice for midrange connected devices, particularly in logistics, metering and smart-city infrastructure.

**LTE Cat-1 bis:** Emerging as a high-volume option as products migrate away from 2G and 3G, especially payment terminals, asset trackers and lightweight telematics devices.
