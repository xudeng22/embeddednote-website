Raspberry Pi, the company whose inexpensive single-board computers helped fuel a generation of maker projects, raised its profit guidance. The company expected to ship more than 4 million units in the first half of 2026, with profitability described as materially ahead of market expectations. Its adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (adjusted EBITDA) were expected to reach at least $38 million.

The update also draws attention to Raspberry Pi 5 pricing. The board uses Broadcom's BCM2712 with a quad-core 2.4 GHz Arm Cortex-A76 CPU and soldered, non-upgradable memory. According to the prices cited in the original article, the range ran from $45 for a 1 GB configuration to $305 for a 16 GB configuration. A board that once sat forgotten in a drawer is starting to look like an unexpectedly expensive piece of kit.
